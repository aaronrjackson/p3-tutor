Comparing in Korean: more than, and the most

By the end of this lesson you can say "I am prettier than you" and "He is faster than his brother" in Korean. You can also say who is the most something.

Objective O1: comparatives and superlatives About 15 minutes Starting point: no Korean words
Why this matters for you

You want to compare people in Korean. All such sentences use one short pattern. Learn the pattern once. Then reuse it with any adjective.

Quality words (adjectives)

A Korean adjective works like a verb. 예뻐요 means "is pretty". So 저는 예뻐요 means "I am pretty". Korean has no extra word for "am" or "is".

The ending -요 makes the sentence polite. Use the polite style with people you do not know well.

A dictionary lists an adjective in a form that ends in 다. The form you say in a sentence is different. The stem changes. Learn the sentence form as one whole word.

One small marker: 는 and 은

A marker is a small word that attaches to the end of a noun. It tells the listener the job of that noun in the sentence. The marker 는 or 은 marks the topic. The topic is the thing you talk about.

  1. After a word that ends in a vowel sound, use 는: 저는, 너는, 그는, 지수는, 사과는.
  2. After a word that ends in a consonant sound, use 은: 형은, 남동생은.

저는 예뻐요.

jeoneun yeppeoyo

I am pretty.

More than: 보다 and 더 5 min

A comparative says one thing has more of a quality than another thing. English changes the adjective: "prettier", "faster". Korean does not change the adjective. Korean adds two small words around it. They are 보다 ("than") and 더 ("more"). (Migaku, How to Study Korean.)

The pattern has four parts, in this order:

  1. The topic with 는/은. This is the thing you say has more.
  2. The other thing with 보다 attached. This is the thing you compare against.
  3. 더.
  4. The adjective in its sentence form.

Examples

저는 너보다 더 예뻐요.

jeoneun neoboda deo yeppeoyo

I am prettier than you. Word for word: I, you-than, more, pretty.

그는 그의 형보다 더 빨라요.

geuneun geuui hyeongboda deo ppallayo

He is faster than his older brother. The word 의 works like "'s". So 그의 형 means "his older brother".

사과는 오렌지보다 더 커요.

sagwaneun orenjiboda deo keoyo

The apple is bigger than the orange.

형은 저보다 더 빨라요.

hyeongeun jeoboda deo ppallayo

My older brother is faster than I am.

Nuances

Nuance 1: 보다 marks the weaker side. The word with 보다 is the thing that has less. In 너보다, "you" has less prettiness than the topic.

Nuance 2: No space before 보다. Write 너보다 as one word.

Nuance 3: You can drop 더. 저는 너보다 예뻐요 is also correct. The 더 makes the "more" clear, so keep it while you learn. (Migaku)

Nuance 4: Korean often drops "my" and "his". People say 그는 형보다 더 빨라요 when the owner is clear. Both forms are fine.

Nuance 5: 덜 means "less". Use 덜 (deol) in the same place as 더. 저는 너보다 덜 빨라요 means "I am less fast than you". (How to Study Korean)

Nuance 6: Women say 오빠. A woman calls her older brother 오빠 (oppa). A man calls his older brother 형. The pattern stays the same.

Nuance 7: 너 is casual. Korean speakers often use a name instead of "you". Use 지수보다 for a friend named Jisu.

Check yourself

Where does 보다 go in a Korean comparison?

Show the answer

Right after that noun. Example: 너보다 means "than you".

Show the answer

The third one. The topic (the faster one) comes first. The 보다 word names the slower one.

The most: 제일 and 가장 3 min

A superlative says one thing has the most of a quality in a whole group. English adds "-est" or "the most". Korean puts 제일 or 가장 right before the adjective. Both words mean the same. (Migaku)

The pattern is: Topic + 제일/가장 + adjective. It has no 보다. A group is the standard, so you need no second noun to compare with.

Examples

지수는 제일 예뻐요.

jisuneun jeil yeppeoyo

Jisu is the prettiest.

형은 가장 빨라요.

hyeongeun gajang ppallayo

My older brother is the fastest.

저는 우리 반에서 제일 커요.

jeoneun uri baneseo jeil keoyo

I am the biggest in our class.

Nuances

Nuance 1: Name the group with 에서. The word 우리 (uri) means "our". The word 반 (ban) means "class". The marker 에서 (eseo) means "in" or "at". So 우리 반에서 means "in our class". Put it before the adjective phrase.

Nuance 2: You can use 이 or 가 instead of 는. Many superlative sentences use the marker 이/가 for the person. Use 가 after a vowel and 이 after a consonant. Example: 지수가 제일 예뻐요. It means the same here.

Nuance 3: Never mix 보다 with 제일 or 가장. "The most" already covers the whole group. Adding "than" makes the sentence awkward. (Sublearn)

Nuance 4: Use 더 for two, 제일 for a group. Use 보다 and 더 to compare one thing with one other thing. Use 제일 or 가장 to pick the top of three or more.

Check yourself

Which word makes "the most" in a Korean sentence?

Show the answer

제일. The word 가장 also works.

Common mix-ups 1 min

A common mix-up

"The adjective should change, as English adds -er and -est." This is tempting because English works this way. In Korean, the adjective stays the same for "pretty", "prettier" and "the prettiest". Compare: 저는 예뻐요 (I am pretty), 저는 너보다 더 예뻐요 (I am prettier than you), 저는 제일 예뻐요 (I am the prettiest). The small words 더, 보다 and 제일 do all the work.

A common mix-up

"보다 goes before the noun, like English 'than'." This is tempting because English says "than you". Korean says "you than". The word 보다 follows the noun. So "than you" is 너보다.

Worked example and practice 3 min

Worked example

Say "Jisu is faster than I am."

  1. Pick the topic. Jisu has more speed, so Jisu is the topic: 지수는. The name ends in a vowel, so use 는.
  2. Pick the thing to compare against. This is "I", with 보다: 저보다.
  3. Add 더.
  4. Add the sentence form of "fast": 빨라요.
  5. Join the parts: 지수는 저보다 더 빨라요.
Your turn

Say "I am bigger than my older brother." Step 1 is done for you: the topic is 저는. Do steps 2 to 5 yourself. The word for "big" is 커요.

Show a solution

Step 2: 형보다. Step 3: 더. Step 4: 커요. Step 5: 저는 형보다 더 커요.

Summary 1 min

  • Comparative: Topic + Other보다 + 더 + adjective.
  • Superlative: Topic + 제일 or 가장 + adjective.
  • The adjective does not change. The small words carry the meaning.
  • 보다 sticks to the end of the thing you compare against.
