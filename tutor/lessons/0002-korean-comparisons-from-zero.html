Korean comparisons from zero: more than, less than, the most

By the end of this lesson you can write "I am prettier than you" and "He is faster than his brother" in Korean. You can also say who is the most something.

Objective O1: comparatives and superlatives About 20 minutes Starting point: no Korean words
Why this matters for you

You want to joke and chat with Korean friends. Friends compare things all the time: who is faster, which food costs more, who is the best. All of these sentences use one short pattern. Learn it once and reuse it with any word.

Warm-up 1 min

Think first

In English, "fast" becomes "faster". "Beautiful" becomes "more beautiful". What changes in the sentence to show the comparison?

Check your answer

English changes the adjective: it adds "-er", or it adds the word "more" before it. Korean does not change the adjective for a comparison. Korean adds small separate words around it. This lesson shows which words and where.

The words you need 6 min

You start with no Korean words, so this section gives every word that the lesson uses. Each word shows the Hangul first, then a sound guide in English letters. The sound guide is only a help.

Quality words (adjectives)

An adjective is a word for a quality, such as "big" or "fast". A Korean adjective also does the job of "is". So 예뻐요 means "is pretty". Korean has no separate word for "am" or "is" before an adjective. Nouns use a word for "is" that a later lesson covers.

A dictionary lists an adjective in a form that ends in 다. You do not say that form in a polite sentence. You say the sentence form, which ends in 요. The ending 요 makes a sentence polite. The part before 다 is the stem, such as 예쁘 in 예쁘다. Some stems change a little before the ending, so learn the sentence form as one whole word.

One small marker: 는 and 은

A marker is a small word that attaches to the end of a noun. It tells the listener the job of that noun in the sentence. The marker 는 or 은 marks the topic, which is the thing you talk about.

  1. After a noun that ends in a vowel, use 는: 저는, 너는, 그는, 지수는, 커피는.
  2. After a noun that ends in a consonant, use 은: 형은, 남동생은.

저는 예뻐요.

jeoneun yeppeoyo

I am pretty.

You will also see the marker 이/가 in comparison sentences, for example in Migaku. It marks the thing that does the action or has the quality. This lesson uses 는/은 and leaves 이/가 for a later lesson.

More than and less than 5 min

A comparative says that one thing has more (or less) of a quality than another thing. Korean builds it with 보다 ("than") and 더 ("more"). (How to Study Korean, Migaku)

The pattern has four parts, in this order:

  1. The topic with 는/은. This is the thing that has more.
  2. The other thing with 보다 attached.
  3. 더.
  4. The adjective in its sentence form.

Examples

저는 너보다 더 예뻐요.

jeoneun neoboda deo yeppeoyo

I am more beautiful than you. Word for word: I, you-than, more, pretty.

그는 그의 형보다 더 빨라요.

geuneun geuui hyeongboda deo ppallayo

He is faster than his older brother. Word for word: he, his older-brother-than, more, fast. The word 의 (ui) works like "'s", so 그의 형 means "his older brother".

그는 그의 남동생보다 더 빨라요.

geuneun geuui namdongsaengboda deo ppallayo

He is faster than his younger brother.

오렌지는 사과보다 더 작아요.

orenjineun sagwaboda deo jagayo

The orange is smaller than the apple.

Nuances

Nuance 1: With 더, 보다 marks the side with less. The noun with 보다 is the thing you compare against. In 너보다 더, "you" has less prettiness than the topic. With 덜 (Nuance 4) the sides swap, because 보다 always marks the thing you compare against.

Nuance 2: No space before 보다. Write 너보다 as one word.

Nuance 3: You can drop 더. 저는 너보다 예뻐요 is also correct. Keep 더 while you learn, because it makes the "more" clear. (How to Study Korean)

Nuance 4: 덜 means "less". Put 덜 where 더 goes. 저는 너보다 덜 빨라요 means "I am less fast than you". Both sources give this pattern. People use 더 far more often than 덜. (Migaku)

Nuance 5: 조금 더 means "a little more". Put 조금 (jogeum, "a little") before 더. 차는 커피보다 조금 더 비싸요 means "Tea is a little more expensive than coffee". (How to Study Korean)

Nuance 6: "His brother" needs a choice. Korean has no plain word for "brother". Say 형 for an older brother or 남동생 for a younger one. Use 그의 형 for "his older brother". Alone, 형 usually means the speaker's own older brother.

Check yourself

Which sentence means "Jisu is bigger than my friend"?

Show the answer

The third one. The topic is the bigger one, and the 보다 noun is the smaller one.

The most 3 min

A superlative says one thing has the most of a quality in a whole group. Korean puts 제일 or 가장 right before the adjective. The two words have almost the same meaning. One source says 제일 sounds a little more casual. (Migaku, How to Study Korean)

The pattern has three parts: Topic + 제일 or 가장 + adjective. It has no 보다 beside one other noun, because the whole group is the standard.

지수는 제일 예뻐요.

jisuneun jeil yeppeoyo

Jisu is the prettiest.

사과는 제일 커요.

sagwaneun jeil keoyo

The apple is the biggest.

Nuances

Nuance 1: Name the group with 에서. The marker 에서 (eseo) after a group means "in" that group. The word 반 (ban) means "class". The word 우리 (uri) means "our". So 우리 반에서 지수는 가장 빨라요 means "In our class, Jisu is the fastest". (Migaku uses 우리 반에서 in the same way.)

Nuance 2: 제일 and 가장 are free to swap. 지수는 가장 예뻐요 has the same meaning as the 제일 sentence above.

Nuance 3: Do not use 보다 with one named thing here. A 제일 or 가장 sentence compares with a whole group, so it normally has no 보다 beside one other noun.

Check yourself

Which sentence means "Coffee is the most expensive"?

Show the answer

The third one. 제일 (or 가장) goes before the adjective and no 보다 appears.

Common mix-ups 2 min

Mix-up 1: changing the adjective

English says "fast" and "faster", so you may want a new form of the adjective. Korean does not do this. 빨라요 stays 빨라요. The small words 더 and 보다 carry the comparison.

Mix-up 2: putting 보다 before the noun

English puts "than" before the noun: "than you". This is why 보다 may feel like it goes first. Korean puts 보다 after the noun: 너보다.

Mix-up 3: using 보다 with 제일 or 가장

You may think a stronger sentence needs both words. It does not. 제일 and 가장 compare against a whole group, and 보다 compares against one thing. Do not pair 제일 or 가장 with 보다 and one named thing.

Mix-up 4: the swapped sides

With 더, the 보다 noun is the side with less. The topic is the side with more. 형은 저보다 더 커요 means "My older brother is bigger than I am". It does not mean that I am bigger.

Mix-up 5: treating 더 and 제일 as the same

더 means "more" and needs something to compare with. 제일 means "the most" and compares with the whole group.

Worked example and practice 3 min

Worked example

Write in Korean: "My friend is faster than I am."

  1. Find the topic. The friend is faster, so the friend is the topic: 친구는.
  2. Find the other side. The comparison is against "I", so write 저 and attach 보다: 저보다.
  3. Add 더.
  4. End with the adjective in its sentence form: 빨라요.
  5. Join the parts in order: 친구는 저보다 더 빨라요.
Your turn

Write in Korean: "Tea is tastier than coffee." The first step is done: the topic is 차는. Write the other three parts.

Show a solution

The other side is coffee: 커피보다. Then 더. Then the adjective 맛있어요. Together: 차는 커피보다 더 맛있어요.

Summary 1 min

  • "More than": Topic는/은 + Other보다 + 더 + adjective.
  • 보다 attaches after the noun. With 더, that noun is the side with less. 덜 replaces 더 for "less".
  • "The most": Topic는/은 + 제일 or 가장 + adjective, with no 보다 beside one other noun.
  • The adjective never changes for a comparison.
